“Knowing yourself is the beginning of all wisdom.”

— Aristotle

When I began my undergraduate journey in Computer Science, artificial intelligence felt like an infinite canvas. Taking Aristotle's reflection to heart prompted me to pause and examine what truly drives me. Looking inward helped me find two complementary forces that shape my work: a sense of purpose rooted in community impact, and a fascination with whether AI systems can be trusted.

My sense of purpose comes from over ten years of volunteering and community teaching with the Associated Initiative for Mankind (AIM) Foundation. Working with underprivileged children in rural and low-bandwidth environments across West Bengal and Odisha grounded my technical outlook in reality. Technology cannot simply be an abstract exercise; it must solve real human problems and work reliably when people depend on it.

That second word, reliably, is what my research is about. A language model can sound trustworthy in a single answer and still fail the second, fifth or fifteenth time it is asked. I study that from two directions:

  • Correctness over repeated use: treating an LLM doing statistical analysis as a production process, and monitoring its error rate with statistical process control (Indian Statistical Institute, Pune).
  • Values under pressure: asking which human values a model gives up first when values collide and a user keeps pushing, across a multi-turn conversation (RespAI Lab, KIIT).

I am also interested in how retrieval and self-reflection could be triggered only when a model's reliability is actually slipping, so that extra cost and latency are spent where they are needed. I am pursuing an MS in Computer Science to deepen this work alongside leading research groups.

Research & Writing

Measuring and monitoring the reliability of large language models.

Preprint · 2026 Indian Statistical Institute (ISI) Pune

Toward a Credible, Accurate and Precise Measurement System for LLM Analytical Errors and Hallucination: Statistical Process Monitoring with CUSUM, EWMA and a Process Reliability Index

Srija Bhattacharyya, under the guidance of Dr Subrata Rath, Head, SQC & OR Unit, Indian Statistical Institute, Pune

Treats an LLM doing statistical analysis as a production process: each response is audited claim by claim against verified reference values, labelled defective or not, and monitored with CUSUM and EWMA control charts and a composite LLM Process Reliability Index. A pilot with three commercial LLMs on three Six Sigma datasets surfaced a counting error near a control limit that recurred across sessions and models, and showed that apparent instability in one dataset was an artefact of pooled improvement stages. The charts' false-alarm and detection rates are characterised by simulation, and a blinded two-rater validation study is specified.

BreakingPoints: Where Does LLM Value Alignment Break?

Five-member team, under the guidance of Dr Murari Mandal (RespAI Research Lab)

Studies where, when and in which direction a language model's value alignment gives way when human values collide and a user keeps pushing across a multi-turn conversation, using Schwartz's ten basic human values as a shared framework and comparing model judgments with human judgments of the same conversations.
